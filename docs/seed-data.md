# Phase 5 — Seed Data

## 1. Purpose

Phase 5 expands the dataset from Phase 1-4's 10-rows-per-table sample into a
dataset large and varied enough to support the analytical SQL planned for
Phase 6 (aggregation, ranking, date analysis, subqueries, `EXISTS`, window
functions) — without weakening any Phase 3/4 constraint.

## 3. Data generation strategy

The dataset was generated by a deterministic Python script (fixed RNG seeds,
not committed to the repo — only its output, `database/seed/01_seed_data.sql`,
is checked in) rather than written by hand or produced with random SQL
functions, per the requirement for a reproducible seed.

**Rows 1-10 of every table are preserved exactly from the original Phase 1
dataset** (same IDs, same relationships) specifically so that every existing
test in `database/tests/phase3_constraint_tests.sql` and
`phase4_relationship_tests.sql` continues to pass unmodified — this was
verified explicitly, not assumed (see §10). Two deliberate exceptions:
`ORDERS`/`PAYMENT` status for rows 1-10 was updated from V1's arbitrary
placeholder values (`PLACED`/`PENDING` etc.) to `DELIVERED`/`SUCCESS`, and
`SYSDATE` was replaced with explicit historical dates — both purely so these
10 rows read coherently as the oldest orders in a ~20-month history, and
neither change affects anything any test depends on.

**`ORDER_ID`/`PAYMENT_ID`/`REVIEW_ID` values `990` and `993`-`999` are
permanently reserved** for the "fresh, nonexistent ID" fixtures already
hardcoded in the Phase 3/4 test files, and are never assigned to real data.
This was a deliberate, explicitly-flagged decision (see the Phase 5 chat
response) rather than editing those tests.

All 30 restaurants were assigned a cuisine "flavor" (via their
`RESTAURANT_CATEGORY` rows), and their menu items are drawn from
category-appropriate name/price pools (e.g. a restaurant tagged Chinese
draws mostly from a Chinese-dish pool, plus generic Beverages/Dessert items
every restaurant realistically also sells).

## 4. Important distributions

**Customer activity** — deliberately uneven, not uniform:
- 5 customers (IDs 96-100) have **zero orders**, guaranteed by construction, not chance.
- The most active customer placed 49 orders; the least active (non-zero) customer placed 3.
- Top 5 by order count: customers 45 and 89 (49 orders each), 82 (46), 78 (44), 79 (41).

**Restaurant popularity** — weighted, not uniform:
- Busiest restaurant: 187 orders. Quietest: 23 orders.
- Top 5: restaurants 5 (187), 4 (185), 1 (178), 3 (156), 2 (142).
- No restaurant has zero orders.

**Order status** (2,000 orders): `DELIVERED` 1,635, `CANCELLED` 333, `ONWAY` 17, `PLACED` 15.
Older orders resolve to `DELIVERED`/`CANCELLED` only; `ONWAY`/`PLACED` are
reserved for orders within ~2 weeks of the dataset's as-of date
(2026-09-25) — an order placed 18 months ago and still showing `PLACED`
would be unrealistic, so status is generated as a function of recency, not
independently of it.

**Order dates**: span 2025-01-06 to 2026-09-25 (~20 months), with mildly
increasing monthly volume (49 orders in 2025-01 rising into the 60s-70s in
later months) to simulate organic growth rather than a flat, artificial rate.

**Review ratings**: skewed positive, matching real-world review behavior —
5★: 256, 4★: 268, 3★: 117, 2★: 79, 1★: 33.

## 5. Historical price snapshot examples

19.83% of all order items (1,065 of 5,370) have an `ORDER_ITEM.PRICE` that
differs from that item's *current* `MENU_ITEM.PRICE` — concentrated in
orders older than ~6 months, simulating a price increase since the order was
placed. Real examples pulled directly from the generated data:

| ORDER_ID | ITEM_ID | Qty | Price at order time | Current MENU_ITEM price |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 13 | 31 | 2 | 204 | 224 |
| 17 | 159 | 1 | 79 | 94 |
| 17 | 161 | 1 | 202 | 229 |
| 19 | 131 | 1 | 166 | 184 |
| 20 | 110 | 2 | 252 | 279 |

This is precisely the scenario `ORDER_ITEM.PRICE` exists to preserve (Phase
4, Deep Dive 1) — none of these historical order totals would be reproducible
by joining to `MENU_ITEM.PRICE` today.

## 6. Order-total and payment validation

Every one of the 2,000 orders' `TOTAL_AMOUNT` was recalculated to exactly
equal `SUM(ORDER_ITEM.QUANTITY * PRICE)` for that order — **including the 10
preserved V1 rows**, 8 of which (orders 2,3,4,5,7,8,9,10) turned out to have
had an incorrect, never-previously-validated `TOTAL_AMOUNT` in the *original*
V1 dataset (e.g. order 2's line item totals 120, but V1 had hardcoded its
`TOTAL_AMOUNT` as 300). This was a real, previously-undetected bug that
predates all constraint work in this project; it's fixed here rather than
carried forward. Every `PAYMENT.AMOUNT` also exactly equals its order's
`TOTAL_AMOUNT`. Verified programmatically against the final generated SQL
file: **zero mismatches across all 2,000 orders and 1,830 payments.**

## 7. Payment coverage (intentional gap)

170 of 2,000 orders (all `CANCELLED`) have **no** `PAYMENT` row at all —
representing an order cancelled before payment was captured. This is
intentional, not a bug: `PAYMENT.ORDER_ID → ORDERS.ORDER_ID` is a one-way FK
(a payment must reference a real order), but nothing requires the reverse —
an order existing without ever having a successful/attempted payment is a
legitimate real-world state. This 170-row gap is exactly why `PAYMENT` sits
at 1,830 rather than 2,000, and is deliberately useful data for a future
"orders without payment" query.

## 8. Customer activity variation

See §4. In addition: every one of the 100 customers has at least one
`ADDRESS` row (even the 5 who never ordered — modeling "signed up, added an
address, never completed a purchase"), and a meaningful subset of customers
have 2-3 addresses (`HOME`, plus `OFFICE`/`OTHER`), so `ORDERS.ADDRESS_ID`
selection isn't trivially "the customer's only address" for every row.

## 9. Restaurant activity variation

See §4. Every restaurant has at least 6 distinct menu items available to
order from (fixing a generation bug caught during this phase — see §10 —
where 5 of the original restaurants would otherwise have been stuck with
exactly 1 orderable item each, artificially capping order-item variety for
some of the platform's most "popular" restaurants).

## 10. Validation performed

**No Oracle instance is available in this environment** (no `sqlplus`, no
Docker) — **I did not execute this seed data against a real Oracle engine**,
stated explicitly rather than claimed. What was performed instead is
structural/fixture validation, run twice: once against the in-memory
generator state, and again by re-parsing the actual final
`database/seed/01_seed_data.sql` file (10,873 lines) — the second pass is
what matters, since it validates what Oracle would actually see:

- **Row counts**: confirmed to match the table in §2, read from the real file.
- **PK uniqueness**: all 12 tables, zero duplicates.
- **UNIQUE constraints**: all 8 (including `UK_PAYMENT_ORDER`, `UK_REVIEW_ORDER`, `UK_ADDRESS_ID_CUSTOMER_ID`), zero duplicates.
- **FK integrity**: all 13 FKs, including the Phase 4 composite `FK_ORDERS_ADDRESS (ADDRESS_ID, CUSTOMER_ID)`, **zero orphans** across 8,845 real rows.
- **NOT NULL**: zero violations across every NOT NULL column.
- **CHECK constraints**: all 17, zero violations (phone length, PIN-code format, price/amount/quantity positivity, rating ranges, status whitelists).
- **Order-total / payment-amount reconciliation**: zero mismatches (§6).
- **Orphan detection**: zero orphans across every one of the 12 relationships (restaurant-category, menu-restaurant, item-menu, address-customer, order-customer/restaurant/delivery-person/address, order_item-order/item, payment-order, review-order).
- **Every order has ≥1 order item**: confirmed, zero exceptions.
- **Order items always reference a menu item from the *same* restaurant as the order**: confirmed, zero cross-restaurant references.
- **Reserved test-fixture IDs (990, 993-999) do not appear in real data**: confirmed absent from `ORDERS`, `PAYMENT`, `REVIEW`.
- **Every existing Phase 3/4 test's specific setup assumption was individually re-checked against the final dataset** (e.g. address 2 still belongs to customer 2, not customer 1; order 1 still has exactly one payment and one review; the `(order=2, item=3)` pair used by a Phase 3 test still doesn't exist) — all still hold.

`database/tests/phase5_data_validation.sql` contains the equivalent SQL
queries for running this same validation directly against a live Oracle
instance, which I could not do myself here.

## 11. Known limitations

- Names, emails, and comments are realistic-looking but fictional/generated, not sourced from any real dataset.
- The "historical price" mechanism is a simple percentage discount applied probabilistically to older order items, not a modeled price-change-event history (there's no `PRICE_HISTORY` table — none was requested, and adding one would be scope creep for this phase).
- `PAYMENT` row count (1,830) is below the "~2,000" target by design (§7), not a shortfall.
- `RESTAURANT_CATEGORY` (67 rows) and `ADDRESS` (162 rows) land at the lower end of their target ranges (60-100, 150-200) rather than the middle — acceptable per the phase brief's own instruction to preserve referential integrity over hitting exact counts.
